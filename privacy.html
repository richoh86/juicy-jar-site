Privacy Policy

Juicy Jar · Last updated 15 August 2026

Juicy Jar asks you for nothing and has no account of you. There is no sign-up, no profile and no analytics. Two parts of the game do reach the internet — the Game Center leaderboard and the rewarded ads that recharge items — and both are optional. Everything else stays on your phone.

What the app keeps on your device

Juicy Jar stores a small amount of information locally so the game works the way you expect:

  • Your best score
  • Your sound and haptics preferences
  • The board of a game in progress, so an interrupted run can be resumed
  • How many undos and bombs you have left in the current run

This lives in the app's own private storage. It is not readable by other apps, it is never sent to us, and it is deleted when you delete Juicy Jar.

Game Center

If you are signed in to Game Center, your score is submitted to the Juicy Jar leaderboard at the end of a run, and you can open the leaderboard from the game-over screen. That is a service of Apple's, not ours: we receive nothing, we have no server, and we cannot see who you are. It is governed by Apple's privacy policy.

Signing out of Game Center on your device stops it. The rest of the game is unaffected.

Advertising

Juicy Jar shows no banners and no interstitials. An ad appears only at a moment you choose it: when you tap to watch one in exchange for an undo, a bomb, or one more life after the jar overflows. Ignore those buttons and you will never see an ad.

Ads are served by Google AdMob. When you watch one, Google receives data from your device in order to deliver and measure it — including a device identifier, your approximate location derived from your IP address, and information about the ad you were shown. This is described in Google's privacy policy and in how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Two things we deliberately do not do:

  • Ads are non-personalised, always. Every request is marked as such. Ads are not selected on the basis of your interests or your history in other apps.
  • We never ask for tracking permission and never read your advertising identifier (IDFA). Juicy Jar shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt, because there is nothing it would be asking for.

Being honest about the limit of that: even a non-personalised ad uses an identifier from your device for frequency capping — so you are not shown the same ad repeatedly — and for aggregate reporting. That is why the consent step below exists.

Your choices in Europe

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, Juicy Jar shows you a consent form before any ad is requested, and asks whether you agree to that use of your device. You can change your answer at any time from the privacy options in the app.

If you decline, or dismiss the form, no ads are requested. The item buttons simply do not appear, and the game plays on its free charges exactly as it always has.

Children

Juicy Jar is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Ads are non-personalised for every player regardless of age.

What the app still does not do

  • No account, sign-in or registration of ours — Game Center is Apple's and is optional
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting
  • No App Tracking Transparency prompt and no advertising identifier
  • No ads you did not ask to watch
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location, camera or microphone
  • No data sold, and none shared with anyone beyond what serving an ad requires

Changes

This policy is updated before any version that changes what the app does is released, and the change is described in that version's release notes. The previous version of this page, accurate up to version 1.1, said the app had no network code at all; the Game Center leaderboard added in 1.1 and the rewarded ads added in 1.2 are the reason it no longer does.
